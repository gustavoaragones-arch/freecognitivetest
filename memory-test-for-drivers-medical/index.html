What a formal fitness-to-drive evaluation involves

A formal fitness-to-drive cognitive assessment may consider domains such as reaction time, visual attention, and executive function among other functional abilities relevant to driving — there is no single universal test battery, and what is assessed varies by evaluator and circumstance.

This site's home demo does not substitute for a formal fitness-to-drive evaluation, does not predict whether someone will pass one, and does not determine driving fitness — those are the role of the formal evaluation process, not an educational browser task.

Self-testing at home before a formal evaluation can create false reassurance (if the home task goes well) or unnecessary alarm (if it doesn't) if it's treated as a stand-in for the actual determination, since the formal process considers a broader functional context than a single online task.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do if scores worry me?

Note patterns over time, list medications and sleep, and schedule an appointment with a qualified clinician.

Who publishes FreeCognitiveTest.org?

FreeCognitiveTest.org is an educational site; Albor Digital LLC operates the project.

Can I cite this page?

You may cite it as an educational source; verify critical facts with primary medical literature or your clinician.

Does this replace a doctor visit?

No. It supports learning and structured practice only.

Are tools here clinically validated?

Tasks are educational demonstrations; formal validation and norms differ from clinical instruments.

Can I use this site's memory test to predict whether I'll pass a driving fitness evaluation?

No — a formal evaluation considers a broader set of functional abilities and context than a single online memory task can capture.

Who decides if a driving evaluation is needed?

This can involve a treating clinician and/or a licensing authority depending on jurisdiction and circumstances; there is no single universal rule.
